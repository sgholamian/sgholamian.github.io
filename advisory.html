Advisory Services

Working with early-stage AI teams, a few hours a week.

I work with teams building AI products, from the first decision about where AI genuinely fits, through to getting a system reliable enough to put in front of customers. The pattern I see most often is a strong prototype that stalls on the way to production: the demo works, but nobody can say whether a change made it better, and the failure modes only surface in front of customers.

I lead an applied AI research organization that has taken agentic AI from research through to production at enterprise scale in heavily regulated domains. Most AI advice comes from startup experience. Mine comes from shipping into environments with auditors, privacy review, model governance, and customers who cannot tolerate a confident wrong answer.

What changes when we work together

A clear view of where AI actually helps

For teams at the start: deciding where AI is genuinely the right tool and where it is not, what to build first, what to buy, and how to sequence a roadmap you can defend to a board or an executive sponsor. I have ideated, pitched, and secured funding for applied research programmes and carried them through to shipped product.

Research that yields IP and publications, not just features

Framing problems so the work produces patentable invention alongside a shipped capability, running invention disclosure and prior-art review with counsel, and taking results through peer review. I hold granted U.S. patents and peer-reviewed publications at leading AI and software engineering venues, and I review for several journals and conferences.

Architecture that holds up, and evaluation that proves it

Where prompting ends and retrieval, fine-tuning, or domain adaptation begins, and how to build multi-agent and reasoning systems that stay debuggable as they grow. The evaluation that tells you whether a change actually helped: versioned datasets, LLM-as-judge and human-in-the-loop scoring, and production traces turned into regression suites that run before release. And the constraints that shape all of it: PII and privacy risk, model governance and Responsible AI review, and on-premises or sovereign deployment when a third-party API is not an option.

The right team, and an honest read on the technology

Hiring research scientists and ML engineers, structuring a small research group, and separating genuine research questions from engineering ones. Also independent assessment of AI capabilities and claims, whether for investors running diligence or for a build, buy, or partner decision.

Who this is for

Teams come to this at very different stages: working out where AI fits in the first place and what the strategy should be, getting a promising prototype to production reliability, or doing genuine research that should produce defensible IP and publishable results.

I do this alongside a full-time role, so engagements are necessarily small in number and clear in scope. The problems I can help with most are the ones where research judgement changes the outcome.

How it works

Most engagements are light and ongoing: a few hours a week, usually a standing call plus asynchronous review of designs, evaluation results, or research plans. Short scoped work also makes sense, such as a one-off architecture review or a diligence exercise. Where the work warrants it, engagements can extend to contributing directly to patent filings and publications, as a named inventor or co-author. Availability, terms, and any IP or authorship arrangements are best discussed directly.
